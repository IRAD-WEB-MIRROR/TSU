About the reckonings

Months. In speech and in official documents the months have Russian-derived names (yanvar, fevral…). The traditional names are used in the Autonomous Tribal Regions, in almanacs and in the Iradi-language press. Five are named after the deer, wild sheep and ibex of the hunt; most of the others are numbered, counting down to Birdin ayı, “the month of the one”: Üçtün ayı, “the month of the three”, is counted from the Pleiades. August is also called Janıl ayı, Janyl's month, after the feast of St. Janyl.

The animal year. The müçöl counts age, not belief. In town the year turns on 1 January; in the traditional reckoning it turns in spring, at Nooruz, and some highland clans count it from Süyünchü Künü, the Annunciation, on 25 March. Older people think the Cow year (uy jılı) unlucky, and some highland grandmothers call it the Year of the Argali (arkar jılı); the Horse year (jılkı jılı) is thought a good one. The twelfth, twenty-fourth, thirty-sixth and later müçöl years of a life are thought dangerous: people give away clothing and ask a priest's blessing. The Church tolerates the custom as long as it is not used for fortune-telling.

The church year. The Syro-Iradi Church has kept the Gregorian calendar since the union with Rome in 1931; before that it kept the Julian calendar, thirteen days behind (twelve before 1900). Its year follows the East Syriac seasons, from Subara four Sundays before the Nativity to Kudash Idta, the Dedication of the Church. Exact dates for each year are published by the Archeparchy in the Tartip; see the calendar of the Church of St. Janyl. Movable feasts here are worked out from the date of Easter; local patronal feasts are not shown.
